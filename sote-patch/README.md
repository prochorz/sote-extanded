# SotE: new controls

A patch for **Star Wars: Shadows of the Empire (USA) (Rev 2)**. It changes Dash's controls in the on-foot levels to a modern scheme: camera-relative movement, a lazy behind-the-back camera, and Z lock-on. Vehicles and ships are untouched. A **Cheats** entry with the game's debug menu appears in the pause menu.

## What changes

| Button | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Stick | forward/back + "tank" turning | Dash runs where the stick is tilted, relative to the camera |
| Camera | hard-locked to his back; on stairs it pitches up (~30°) or down | lazy: it follows behind Dash as he runs; running toward the camera doesn't spin it; if Dash stands ~1.5 s it eases back behind him; on stairs and slopes it stays level |
| Z (no enemy near) | aim mode | native aim + the camera goes behind him; if another view is selected with Camera (L) (first person, the tripod camera, overhead), Z first restores the normal behind camera (on foot and on the train) |
| Z (enemy in view) | aim mode | **Z-targeting**: locks the nearest enemy ahead (if none ahead, an enemy very close behind, up to ~48 units: Dash turns to it), Dash faces it, stick sideways = strafing a circle around the target (the radius holds), forward/back = closer/farther, shots (B) fly at the target; a target above or below Dash (on a rock, in a pit) — the camera tilts toward it (up to 30° up, 20° down; on foot) and levels out when Z is released |
| R + stick | strafe | unchanged |
| Edge of a drop | Dash falls | if he goes off an edge without a jump, and below him is an abyss or a drop of twice his height or more (the second-floor catwalks) — he grabs (to drop on purpose, jump with A) the edge where he went off, and hangs by one hand (both under a wall and under a thin floor). In a jump he grabs the edge of a wall ahead if his hands reach it and he doesn't make it onto it (the camera goes to the side at 40°, on the side where it has room; if there is none — behind him); stick up climbs out, stick down lets go: over an abyss he keeps falling, over a floor he hops off like a normal jump. Jumping off the edge works as before, and a narrow gap Dash clears at a run is cleared as in the original. Details and the rolled-back "never fall" variant: [docs/ledge-falls.md](docs/ledge-falls.md) |

The buttons in the table are for the **Standard** preset. The patch takes the buttons from the game's current preset (Options → Controls), so lock-on always hangs on the "Aim/Look" action:
- Standard (A jump, B fire), Traditional and Alternate (B jump, A fire): Aim/Look = Z, Strafe = R;
- Strafe: Aim/Look = C►, so lock-on is on C► too; Z and R there strafe left/right, and the patch doesn't touch them.
| A, B, C buttons | — | unchanged |

Works on **Ord Mantell Junkyard** too (Dash on the train): there the game has a separate player class (`HumT`) with different fields and faster turning, and the patch supports both.

Bosses lock on too (checked on the AT-ST in Echo Base and IG-88 on Ord Mantell): shots fly at the AT-ST's weak point at the top, i.e. the cockpit, with every weapon — laser, Pulse and the rest of the projectiles go there (checked laser, Pulse and Seeker; Pulse dies on its own after ~30 units, under a lock it flies to the target, though it does less damage from afar than up close), and the Seeker homes on the cockpit (the game's native auto-aim skips bosses, so missiles and Pulse used to fly chest-high into its legs). IG-88 lunging up close stays locked (its field `+0x60` turns negative at that moment, which the patch used to take for death). The gladiator in Xizor's Palace locks onto the lowest still-intact armour: in the fight that's the body, and when it flies it's the head (the aim used to sit on the shins). Underwater in the sewers the patch now works (swimming sets a flag on Dash that the patch took for death and switched off): Z locks the dianoga's eye (a boss), not the tentacles; when the eye hides in the mouth, Z takes a tentacle, and a new press returns the lock to the eye as soon as it comes out. A boss ahead outranks normal enemies: in the second phase Z used to grab the stormtroopers in the maze below Dash instead of the gladiator flying ~60 units away. An enemy can be locked no farther than 150 units (~25 stormtrooper heights; the game's native auto-aim takes the same) and only if it's visible through the level fog at least as a faint silhouette (the fog hides at most 90%; in the first part of Echo Base, where the fog is thick, that's ~90 units — the turrets shoot from there). The lock drops when you release Z, the target dies, moves past 240 units, vanishes in the fog (>97%) or hides behind a wall or train car for a second. If Z is still held then, Dash doesn't switch to the native aim (where the stick would pitch the gun) but stays turned the same way: stick sideways = strafe, back = back away, forward = run ahead; as soon as a new enemy appears ahead, the lock takes it on its own. The native aim is switched on by a new press of Z when there's no target. **Switching target:** release Z and press it again quickly (within ~0.4 s) — the aim moves to the next target in priority order (for the dianoga: eye → tentacles in turn → eye again); a slow press takes the best target. Enemies behind walls are not locked (the game's native auto-aim doesn't check walls at all); a target can be blocked by level geometry, crates and train cars, not by items or other enemies in front of it (the Challenge Point icon in a doorway used to stop a clearly-visible enemy from being locked). On the train, the drones (`TGun`), targets (`TTar`) and car droids (`TTrp`) — which the game keeps apart from the rest of the enemies — lock on too.

While a target is locked you see it on screen: black bars slide in top and bottom, and a frame of four yellow triangles stands around the enemy. At the moment of locking, the triangles fly together onto the target in a spiral.

## Cheats in the pause menu

In the pause menu, below Resume / Options / Quit (and Game Secrets, if open), there's a **Cheats** entry. It's the game's own developer debug menu (which the GameShark code `800D098B 0001` used to enable), with all the items (Next / Prev Level merged into Go to level, plus Reset; Screen Resolution removed: on an emulator the resolution is set by GLideN64 and the item did nothing), but with normal controls:
- stick/D-pad up-down — pick an item, **A** — run or toggle, **L / R** — less / more (holding accelerates), **B** or START — back to the pause menu;
- toggles and numbers (Invincibility, Wall Ghost, Jump Power, Gravity, fog, FOV, lights, etc.) change in place, the game stays paused, the current value shows on the right;
- **Invincibility**: damage doesn't get through (on foot, on the train, on the speeder). As in the original, it lasts until the end of the level: on a new level Dash is new and the row shows OFF (the menu reads Dash's own state, not the item's flag). It doesn't save you from falling into a pit. Ships and the snowspeeder have no invincibility;
- **Jetpack Fuel** (our item, right below Invincibility): while on, the jetpack's tank is topped up every frame, so it never runs dry (the fuel is a float on the on-foot player — Humn +0xBC — that the game burns while the jetpack fires and clamps to 100; a full tank lasts ~12 s). As the cheat marks the fuel percentage in the HUD blue — the blue Invincibility gives the health number — the number itself says the cheat is on. It's off by default and otherwise touches nothing: switch it off and the tank drains as usual, and on a level without a jetpack (where the HUD element slot means something else) the row does nothing;
- Get all stuff, 50 Lives, Revert Fog and Lights run in place ("done" appears on the right);
- **Go to level** instead of Next / Prev Level: L/R pick any of the 32 sub-levels (the name shows at the bottom, e.g. "4a. Train" or "Cut 03 (Train)" for a cutscene), A loads it. So you can jump several levels at once and skip cutscenes you can't open the pause from;
- Kill Dash, Teleport, This Level and Go to level unpause and run at once;
- **Reset cheats** (always the last item; the right shows how many settings changed) restores everything changed in this menu to its value before the first change. Given things (weapons, 50 lives) are not taken away; fog, FOV and lights roll back only if the level hasn't changed (a new level sets its own anyway);
- **Ship** (our item, right below Revert Fog and Lights): picks the player ship on Skyhook — Outrider, X-Wing or TIE Fighter, the game's secret #2 — without the secret unlocked and without holding C-right. L / R step back / forward, A steps forward, and the camera blends behind the new ship as with the native swap. The row shows the game's own state: a restart of the level (death, This Level) brings back the Outrider, as in the original, and the row then reads Outrider. Reset cheats puts back the ship from before the first change. Only Skyhook's ship model has the three variants: on any other level the row reads "(Skyhook)" and does nothing. The value column fits ~10 characters, so the TIE Fighter reads "TIE".
- **Leebo Scanner** (our item, below Ship): enables the game's secret #1 — the top-down view, from which enemies and pickups are visible. In the normal game it needs C-right held ~5 s and the secret unlocked (a code in the profile name or the game beaten); here the item switches it straight on, and off restores the previous view. The row shows the real state: change the camera view (L) and it reads OFF. On foot only; under a low ceiling, like the secret itself, it shows the normal behind camera.
- **Creature Cam** (our item, below Leebo Scanner): what the profile name " Wampa  Stompa" gives, picked from the row instead of the name and its buttons (the game has them only in the Traditional control preset; the row works in every preset, nothing held). L / R step back / forward, A steps forward. On foot: **Dash** / **Wampa** / **Trooper** — the camera follows the first wampa (or the first trooper) of the level from behind while Dash stays the player, and the D-pad drives the creature in any control preset (as the game's own mode does in Traditional: D-up walks the wampa, D-left / D-right turn the wampa or the trooper, D-down makes the wampa attack; the current preset's D-pad is given as in Traditional while a creature is followed, put back at Dash, so Dash's own D-pad walk does not move him meanwhile); the stick and B drive it too: stick up walks, stick left / right turns, B attacks, while Dash stands (the stick, A and B are taken from him; the D-pad, Z, L, R, C and START stay as they are); the row asks the creatures the way the game does, so where none answers it stays at Dash. It reads the game's own state: a new level or a new Dash (death) brings the camera back to Dash and the row reads Dash. In Battle of Hoth: **Speeder** / **AT-ST** — AT-ST puts the camera behind the first AT-ST by itself (the game's own view for it) as soon as that AT-ST is there (its wave is in; before it the camera stays on the speeder, and it goes back there when the AT-ST sleeps or is destroyed) and hands the AT-ST to the D-pad (D-left / D-right turn it, D-up picks a target and fires) and to the stick and B: stick left / right turns it (about twice as fast as the D-pad at a full push, slower with a lighter push), stick forward walks it, faster the further it is pushed (a full push is the game's own walk speed), centre or back stops it (it finishes its step and stands with both feet down, and can still turn and fire), B fires straight where it faces (no auto-aim), and with Z held its head takes the nearest enemy ahead (a probe droid, another AT-ST or an AT-AT, never a speeder or a rebel gun) and the bolts go there; the camera stays behind it, higher up and looking down ahead of it (the whole AT-ST in the lower half of the screen, its shots and what is ahead in view), and swings round after it when it turns, in place too, by the game's own rule for the camera behind Dash; the snowspeeder gets no stick, A, B or Z meanwhile and waits where it was, kept whole (nothing hurts it there), flying on from there with Speeder; while it is on the D-pad of the current preset is given as in Traditional (put back on Speeder, Reset cheats or another level). Speeder lets the AT-ST walk on its own AI again and the camera goes back to the speeder. Elsewhere the row reads "(none)" and does nothing. Reset cheats puts back the value from before the first change.
- **Friendly Wampas** (our item, below Creature Cam, off at boot): the game's secret #4 — a wampa that goes for Dash calms down instead of attacking. The game gives it only for beating the game on Jedi (a profile-name code cannot open it); here the row makes the game's own check answer yes at the wampa code's three checks, so the game's own calm-down runs. It stays on through level changes and deaths, Reset cheats turns it back off. On a profile that beat Jedi the secret is on anyway and OFF cannot turn it off. Nothing happens on a level without wampas.
- **New Controls / Z Lock-On / Ledge Hang** (the first three items) switch the patch's features off: off means the stock game's behaviour (native controls and camera, Z is native aiming, no edge grab). After the game starts everything is on again; Reset cheats turns them back on too. Without New Controls the lock-on is off too (shows OFF). Switching off mid-action releases everything cleanly: the lock drops, Dash falls from the edge.
- Dir Light Vec: L/R — turns the light direction, C-up/down — pitch;
- **Gall Run (bonus)** (in the release since 2026-10-24; left out only by `build.py --without gallrun`): level 32 in **Go to level** (the list has 33 entries in a build with it, the last "3. A Gall Run (bonus)"; no row of its own): a bonus mission based on the comic: an opening shot (fade in from black, the Outrider and the Falcon enter the canyon), then stage 1, the canyon run, where you fly the Falcon behind the Outrider, and stage 2, the gunner, where you shoot TIEs from the turret over open terrain. Dash, Lando and Chewie talk over the radio. The game's own levels are unchanged.

Outside the pause the module does nothing: the game's debug flag is not touched, so Z, A, B and the D-pad in the game work as always. **The GameShark code `800D098B 0001` must be turned off** — with it the game runs the old menu's items on every press, as before.

## Install

Get the BPS patch (from this repository's Releases, or `local/build/sote-patch/sote-patch.bps` made by the build
below) and apply it to your own ROM with Floating IPS or Rom Patcher JS. The source ROM must be exactly
`Star Wars - Shadows of the Empire (USA) (Rev 2).z64`, SHA-256 `e7085e01…642c`. The patched ROM's MD5 starts with
`7DC1ECE7`, so a save made with an earlier version carries over. Open it in M64Plus FZ (or any mupen64plus-based
emulator).

**The Expansion Pak (8 MB of RAM) is required.** In M64Plus FZ it's on by default. If the game hangs on a black screen, check the game settings that "Disable Expansion Pak" is off. On real N64 hardware without the Expansion Pak the patch won't work.

## Building from source

Needs: Homebrew `llvm`, `lld`, Python 3, and your own copy of the game (USA Rev 2 ROM).

No game data is in this repository: the ROM, the harness savestates (they hold the console's RAM), the game's code
dump `local/work/main.bin` and the assets made from the game (`local/game/falcon.bin`, `local/game/gall_run.bin`,
`local/game/gall_level*.bin`) are made locally from your ROM by the scripts below and are git-ignored.

From a fresh clone, one command makes all of it from your ROM (needs mupen64plus from Homebrew and numpy too;
~15 min, each part skipped when present, one OK line at the end), then the build:

```bash
tools/setup.sh "<your rom>.z64"        # from the repo's top
cd sote-patch && python3 build.py "../local/rom/Star Wars - Shadows of the Empire (USA) (Rev 2).z64"
```

`setup.sh` copies the ROM to `local/rom/`, builds the harness's dylibs, boots the game on the harness to make the
savestates the assets come from (`harness/bootstrap.py`), writes `local/work/main.bin` (`harness/make_main.py`) and
the assets below. A release built from them (`tools/release.sh`) keeps the `7DC1ECE7` MD5 prefix; it is not
byte-equal to the published ROM, only in the Gall Run image: Dash's two poses (his idle animation follows the whole
game state of the state they are made from, `harness/states_dash.py`), the gun towers' turn as copied from their source
state (39 bytes; the mission sets each tower's yaw every frame) and a 2-byte header pair in each of the 11 face sprites
(Dash, Luke, Lando, Leia). The mission's checks pass on both ROMs and their shots look the same. The steps one by one:

The default build also needs the Falcon model `local/game/falcon.bin` (the `falcon` feature, the bonus mission's ship, enabled together with `gallrun`). In the ROM it's stored compressed, so it's taken from the game's RAM on the harness: `cd ../harness && M64CPU=0 python3 falcon_asset.py "../local/rom/Star Wars - Shadows of the Empire (USA) (Rev 2).z64"`. The script loads `local/states/src_model.st` (the ship-model source), copies the Falcon's mesh, moves all pointers to `0x80500000`, and scales (×0.6) and turns (180°) the vertices so the nose points along the direction of travel, like the Outrider.

The `gallrun` feature (together with `falcon`) is part of the default build and the release (`--without gallrun` leaves both out). It needs `local/game/gall_run.bin`: `cd ../harness && M64CPU=0 .venv/bin/python3 gallrun/gall_asset.py "<rom>"`. The script takes the geometry from RAM dumps of the levels (`local/states/src_*.st`, Gall's textures), the routes from `harness/gallrun/`, the lines, Dash's portrait, Chewie's sound and the opening-shot keys. With `--without gallrun` neither file is needed.

`--md5-prefix 7DC1ECE7` picks 8 bytes at the end of the ROM so its MD5 starts with those digits (1–3 min of search, `tools/md5prefix.c`). Emulators on mupen64plus name the save `<name>-<first 8 hex digits of MD5>.eep`, so after a patch update the old save is picked up on its own. The loader copies a fixed 64 KB, so the first megabyte of the ROM and the CRC in the header also don't change between builds.

## How it works

- The game's main code in the ROM is compressed, so the patch changes only the uncompressed loader. It DMA-copies our code (up to 64 KB) to `0x807C0000`, zeroes `.bss` (variables without initial values; they're not in the ROM, they sit in RAM right after the code), loads the sections that live outside the payload (below), puts the hooks into the already-decompressed game code and hands control to the game. The ROM checksum (CIC 6102) is recomputed.
- Hooks (see `src/hooks.S`):
  - `0x800758A8`: after the stick decode in Dash's control function `0x80074FA4`. Here the stick is turned into a camera-relative direction; the Z-targeting runs through this hook too.
  - `0x800778D0` / `0x800785A8`: before the turn-rate integration (on the ground / in the air). This is where the quick turn to the wanted direction comes from.
  - `0x8006282C` / `0x80062AFC` / `0x80062C8C`: before the "Doly" camera modes. Here the turn in the player's matrix copy is replaced with the lazy camera angle.
  - `0x800321FC`: laser bolt creation. Here the shot's direction is aimed at the locked target.
  - `0x80073D44`, `0x800741B8`, `0x800741E0`, `0x80074208`, `0x80074510`, `0x80074538`, `0x80074838`: the projectile spawn calls `0x80064584` in Dash's fire function (every weapon except the laser and the Seeker; the Pulse is `0x80074838`): the velocity is turned to the target.
  - `0x8007E970`: the Seeker missile's target (the native auto-aim result): under a lock it becomes the locked target.
  - `0x80064604`, `0x800646A0`: the projectile lifetime write in the spawn: a shot just aimed at the locked target lives long enough to reach it (up to 4 s).
  - `0x800AAFC0` / `0x800AC9E0`: the same stick-decode and turn hooks in Dash's on-train control function `0x800AAADC` (class `HumT`), just with the `train` function. The class differences (heading fields, stack slots, death flag) are described in the `KINDS` table in `core.c`, the train input gating in `train.c`.
  - `0x800175B8`: `set_anim`'s entry (the shared animation function): while Dash hangs on an edge, his pose is replaced here.
  - `0x8007A104`: the end of Dash's control method: holding him on the edge (position, model turn) after the game's physics.
  - `0x80072324`: the "fire now?" check in Dash's weapon code: while he hangs or falls from a hang, the game takes the "button not pressed" branch.
  - `0x800153D4` / `0x80024854` (the `cheats` function): pause input handling (before the game's own) and pause screen drawing (after the game's). Details in the `cheats.c` header. `0x80009138` (the game's HUD text colour setter, a function entry): the Jetpack Fuel cheat draws the fuel number blue.
  - `0x8009C790`, `0x8009C3F0`, `0x8009C5F8` (the `falcon` feature, only with `gallrun`): in the `Stft` ship's draw method, after a matrix is written into a gun node, the model root or the engine-glow node. Here the guns are moved onto the Falcon's turrets, the glow onto its stern, and the drawn ship by the Gall Run's offsets.
  - `0x8001311C` (the `gallrun` and `cheats` features): the raw controller read (the mission takes the stick before the game decodes it; Creature Cam turns the stick and B into the followed creature's D-pad). `0x80009648` (`gallrun`): the game's camera switch (refused in the mission's free flight).
  - `0x80099AC8` (+ nop at `0x80099ACC`) and `0x8009BF3C` (the `gallrun` feature): the `Stft` ship's view-key and gun gates, which read the level's sector record `*0x800D2400` (the space level has one, the base level none). While the mission's free flight runs (`g_sky_on`) the trampolines take the gates as open, so the Falcon gets the space level's view keys (cockpit / behind), its own lasers and missiles; elsewhere they do what the game did.
  - The sub-level tables' users `0x800192DC`..`0x80019518` and the level-index table `0x80035D64` / `0x80035D74` (the `gallrun` feature): the game's loader, name getter and save index read our 33-entry tables (`g_levels`, `g_level_index`), so the Gall Run is sub-level 32 of its own.
  - `0x800B1DB4` (the `gallrun` feature): the enemy shot's damage scale by level; the Gall Run keeps the base level's.
  - `0x80027854` and `0x800194DC` (the `gallrun` feature): the restart's re-queue of the current level (32 would go to the Main Menu past the game's bound) and the level queue itself, where every request from the chain's last scene (31) but Cut 06 becomes Cut 06.
  - `0x80082494` (the `gallrun` feature): the `PRBT` tower's heal store, skipped in the Gall Run.
  - `0x800BB864`: closing the scene's frame display list (after the HUD). Here the lock-on bars and frame are drawn: fill rectangles in fill mode, triangles decomposed into rows. The enemy's on-screen position is computed by projection through the active camera (fovy ≈ 77°, 4:3). `falcon_frame` is called from here once per frame too.
- `gallrun`: `gallrun.c` and `gall_*.c` link into the `.gall` section at `0x80600040` (the ~160 KB before its image, outside the 64 KB payload), and the image `local/game/gall_run.bin` sits from `0x80628000`; `_start` loads both in one DMA. The mission's level, `local/game/gall_level.bin` (the base level's blob, edited), follows them in the ROM uncompressed; it is sub-level 32 of its own: the game's sub-level tables are grown to 33 entries in `g_levels` (`gallrun.c`, copied at boot by `gallrun_boot()`; the users' instructions rewritten in `hooks.S`), the base level's own entry is never touched. While it runs, `Trak`'s and `TieF`'s class methods are wrapped too (put back after). In mission mode the mission's code swaps the `Chor` route for the route from the image each frame, hangs the stage geometry on a rock's node, draws the Outrider, steers the Falcon (stage 1) and the TIEs (stage 2), the Trak camera and the opening shot, plays the end scene at Slave I's pad and the chase up into space, and sets Gall's fog and lights.
- `falcon` (built together with `gallrun`): `build.py` appends `local/game/falcon.bin` (~122 KB) to the ROM after the payload and the cheats slot (`0xC18000`), and `_start` DMA-copies it to `0x80500000` at load. That's a free part of the Expansion Pak, which the game's levels leave unused. The mission's level has the Falcon in its own data: `harness/gallrun/level_edit.py` puts the Falcon's mesh node in the ship model's group in the Outrider hull's place, without the Outrider's engine glows. `falcon.c` does only what the game rebuilds every frame. The two Outrider guns (top and bottom) stay; their nodes are built each frame by the `Stft` draw method: the aim rotation plus a z +5.2 / −5.0 offset. The hooks on its matrix copies (`jal 0x8000FE48`) move that offset onto the Falcon's turret rings, so the guns aim as before, and the pulsing glow onto the Falcon's stern. The mesh format is described in `docs/reverse-engineering.md`.
- The code is split into modules (`src/`):
  - `core.c`: camera-relative movement, turning, the lazy camera (level on slopes), Z restores the behind camera;
  - `lock.c`: Z lock-on (target selection, circling, fog, bosses, aiming shots) and its picture: the bars and the reticle (`ov_color` / `fill_tri` / `project` can be drawn by other features too);
  - `common.h`: shared (math, player classes, declarations between modules); `game.h`: the game's addresses and fields.
  - Optional features, each in one file with its own hooks:
    - `hang.c` (`hang`): the edge hang;
    - `train.c` (`train`): the Ord Mantell train: control of the `HumT` class, drones and droids as targets, the train camera;
    - `cheats.c` (`cheats`): the Cheats pause entry. Its code and data link into the `.cheats` section at `0x80540000` (between the Falcon model and `.gall`), outside the 64 KB payload: a fixed 32 KB ROM slot right after the payload (`0xC10000`, `CHEATS_SPACE`, there in every build, zeros `--without cheats`), DMA-copied by `_start` first of the three loads; its `.bss` stays in the payload's. Built with `-Oz`, game functions and variables are absolute `GAME()` symbols;
    - `falcon.c` (`falcon`): the bonus mission's ship — the Falcon instead of the Outrider (model `local/game/falcon.bin`; built only with `gallrun`);
    - `gallrun.c` (`gallrun`): the Gall Run bonus mission (an entry in Cheats; `local/game/gall_run.bin`): entry points and frame glue; with it `gall_scene.c` (the rock nodes it draws with), `gall_fly.c` (stage 1 flight), `gall_story.c` (the cast and the story), `gall_gun.c` (stage 2's gunner: the sight, the target panel, friendly fire), `gall_space.c` (the chase's free flight), `gall_native.c` (the final battle on the game's own handling), `gall_ties.c` (TIEs, battle, bombers, finale), `gall_cam.c` (the camera), and `gall.h`, their internal header (shared defines, state, functions; included only by them, after `common.h`). All built with `-Oz`.

  `python3 build.py <rom> --without hang` (or `--without train` / `--without cheats`, several allowed; `--without gallrun` leaves `falcon` out too) builds the patch without a feature: its code and its hooks don't go into the ROM, and the core calls empty stubs from `common.h`. Without `train`, the train keeps the game's native controls. How to add a new feature — below, in "New feature: checklist".
- The settings sit at the top of each module: the stick dead zone in `common.h`, turning and camera in `core.c`, lock range in `lock.c`, the hang in `hang.c`.

### New feature: checklist

The feature name is `<name>`, the flag is `FEATURE_<NAME>`.

1. **Code**: `src/<name>.c`. The payload with everything ≤ 64 KB (`PAYLOAD_SPACE`, ~52 KB used now; `cheats.c` and the Gall Run's code are outside it); if it doesn't fit — a line in `SIZE_OPT` in `build.py` (`-Os`/`-Oz`, but not for per-frame code: it changes the game's frame time) or its own section outside the payload, like `.gall` in `gallrun` or `.cheats` in `cheats`.
2. **`build.py`**: a line in `FEATURES` (every feature there is in the default build and the release; `--without <name>` leaves it out).
3. **`common.h`**: declarations under `#if FEATURE_<NAME>`, and in the `#else` — empty `static inline` stubs for every call from the core (`core.c`, `lock.c`), so a `--without <name>` build compiles.
4. **`hooks.S`**: entries in `hook_table` (`.word <address>` + `jal`/`j <trampoline>`) and the trampolines, all under `#if FEATURE_<NAME>`. Every address the feature changes in the game code must be in `hook_table`: `harness/inject.py` (`all_hooks()`) reads it from there and puts the game's native instructions back when the feature is off, so savestates from older builds stay usable.
5. **Data** (if any, like `local/game/gall_run.bin`): append to the ROM in `build.py`, DMA-copy in `_start` (`hooks.S`) and in `inject()` (`harness/inject.py`), because tests load a savestate, not the ROM. The data generator goes in `harness/`.
6. **Cheats** (if the feature is to be toggled in the game): a line in `cheats.c` under `#if FEATURE_<NAME>` and a fix to `OURS` (how many of our items sit above the game's).
7. **Tests**: `harness/tests/t_<name>.py` (first import is `import _path`), a line in `SUITE` in `harness/run_all.py`, module → tests in the `sote-test` skill table (`.claude/skills/sote-test/SKILL.md`).
8. **Docs**: the module and its hooks — in "How it works" above, a line about the module in `../CLAUDE.md`, "Verified" — when the tests pass.
9. **Check**: build with the feature and with `--without <name>`, run its tests and the tests of the modules it touches (`run_all.py <names>`).

General knowledge about the game (addresses, player classes, how to inject code and test on the harness) is in [../docs](../docs).

Thanks to [SotE-Recomp](https://github.com/GTTeancum/SotE-Recomp): some addresses come from its reverse engineering; the rest were found by RAM probing in mupen64plus.

## Demo

The videos (not in this repository): the original, the patch, and both side by side (original left, patch right). The route on Escape from Echo Base is the same, the stick and pressed buttons show at the bottom. Recorded by a script on the harness (`tools/make_demo_video.py`).

While recording the demo two Z-targeting bugs were found and fixed:
- **The lock dropped when Dash was hit.** The game blocks controls for a moment (stun), and the patch reset the target then. Now the lock survives hits.
- **A visible enemy couldn't be locked.** The visibility check cast its ray too low, and it caught on thresholds. Now the ray goes at the top of the enemy's body, from Dash's chest or the camera.

## Known limits

- The full ROM MD5 changes with every update (only the first 8 digits are kept, see "Build"). If some emulator keys saves by the full MD5, the save has to be renamed.
- On the harness the reticle frame is visible only with the visibility check off, because the nearest stormtroopers stood behind a wall. In the game it appears only over an enemy that's visible.
- Near walls the camera can come closer than before: the wall push-back stayed native.
- The blaster's barrel in the hand doesn't visually tilt toward the target, though the bolts themselves fly at it.
- The Falcon is just a different model (plus the Outrider's guns on its turrets). The collision, the points the lasers come from, the ship icon in the HUD and the level's final cutscene stay the Outrider's (the last two weren't checked yet). It's the game's own model, made to be seen from afar, so up close it's simple (16 parts).
